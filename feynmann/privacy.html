We're with you

How Feynmann looks after what you share with it. By Rushabh Parikh. Last updated 5 October 2026.

Learning means getting things wrong out loud. You should be able to do that here without worrying about who's watching. So here, in plain words, is what we keep, why we keep it, and how you stay in charge of it.

When you sign in with Google

We receive your name, email address and profile picture. We use them to know it's you, so your topics are yours and nobody else's, on every device you use.

What we keep, and why

We save your learning to your account, so you can pick up exactly where you left off and so Feynmann gets better at teaching:

  • Your topics and progress: the steps mapped out for you, the ideas, which steps you've finished, your final-check scores, and what's coming back for review.
  • Your conversations with your Feynmann: how you explained each idea back, the questions you asked, your "what if…" questions, and Feynmann's replies. These are the heart of how you learn here, and they show us where Feynmann explains things well and where it should do better.
  • What you search for and how far you get (the topics you look up, the steps Feynmann maps out, warm-up results, steps finished), so we understand what people want to learn.
  • Feedback you send us, and whether you join the waitlist.
  • How many free credits you've used, to keep things fair for everyone.

All of this is stored privately on Feynmann's servers (Cloudflare, and a private Google Sheet for the activity list). Only the app's owner can see it. We read it to make Feynmann a better teacher, never to judge you. Getting something wrong is how learning starts.

What we'll never do

  • Sell your data, or share it with advertisers. There are none.
  • Show your name, your answers or your conversations to other learners.
  • Keep your OpenAI or YouTube API keys. If you add your own, they stay in the browser you typed them into and are passed straight to OpenAI or YouTube when you use them, without being stored.

Topics others are exploring

When Feynmann maps out a topic, its steps, ideas, checks and suggested videos are shared under topics others are exploring, so the next person curious about it can start straight away, for free. They're shared without your name, and without anything you answered, wrote or said. We privately note who started each topic, only so we can take one down if it's ever inappropriate. Ideas written around your particular mistakes, and the quick tangents you ask for, stay yours alone.

On your device

Your browser keeps a copy of your topics so the app opens instantly. The copy on our servers is the one that counts: sign in anywhere and it's all there.

Visit counts

We count visits with GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly analytics tool: which pages are opened, the site you came from, and your browser, screen size and country. It uses no cookies, isn't linked to your account, and never sees what you type or say.

You're in charge

Want to see what we have, or have all of it deleted? Email rushabhparikh10@gmail.com and we'll take care of it: your topics, your conversations, and every record tied to your email. No forms, no questions asked.

Requests to OpenAI and YouTube are handled by those companies under their own privacy policies.
